> **Silver Week 2026:** September 19–23 is a rare and irregular five-day holiday sequence, the first equivalent run since 2015. Use the holiday for neighbourhood exploration and reserve Asakusa, Ueno, Akihabara and the main shopping block for Thursday, September 24.

## Day 1: Saturday, Sep 19: arrival

Arrive, settle into Akasaka and keep the first evening pressure-free.

- **14:30** Arrive at Tokyo Haneda Airport.
- **Approximately 15:30–16:30** Complete immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Allow up to two hours before becoming concerned.
- At the airport, activate the pre-arranged eSIM or collect pocket Wi-Fi, set up Mobile Suica or obtain an available physical IC card, and withdraw approximately **¥20,000–30,000** as initial cash.
- **Immediately after clearing the airport** Travel to Akasaka by Keikyu/Metro or monorail/Metro. Allow 45–60 minutes and approximately ¥700.
- **Approximately 16:15–17:45** Check into Hotel Monterey Akasaka, freshen up and see how tired you feel.
- **18:30: Izakaya dinner near the hotel:** An izakaya is a relaxed Japanese pub built around drinks and shareable dishes; try yakitori (grilled chicken skewers), seasonal small plates and draft beer. Ordering can scale to your appetite, and staying in Akasaka avoids unnecessary travel after the flight.
- **18:30: Optional high-energy alternative, Omoide Yokocho:** A maze of tiny, post-war-style yakitori counters beside Shinjuku Station, with a smoky, close-quarters old-Tokyo atmosphere. Try negima (chicken and spring onion), tsukune (chicken meatball) and kawa (chicken skin).
- **20:00: Optional, Golden Gai:** Several lanes of minute, highly individual bars in Shinjuku. Go for the unusual micro-bar atmosphere; drinks will not necessarily be cheap. Choose a clearly foreigner-friendly bar and expect a possible ¥500–1,000 cover charge.

Akasaka is the recommended plan if jet lag is noticeable. Omoide Yokocho will be particularly busy on the opening Saturday of Silver Week, and Golden Gai remains entirely optional.

## Day 2: Sunday, Sep 20: hidden Tokyo neighbourhoods, Silver Week

Older residential Tokyo, shrine lanes, bookshops, retro cafés and a focused electronics-store visit. This is a full day.

- **08:30: Quick breakfast:** Pick up onigiri (filled rice triangles), fruit and coffee from a konbini, one of Japan's well-stocked convenience stores. It is a quick local breakfast before a full walking day.
- **09:00: Nezu Shrine:** A historic Shinto shrine complex with wooded grounds and a small tunnel of vermilion torii gates. Start here for a quiet shrine visit before exploring the surrounding older neighbourhoods.
- **10:00: Yanaka:** A low-rise district of temples, wooden houses and quiet lanes that survived much of Tokyo's twentieth-century destruction. Walking there shows a residential, slower-paced counterpoint to the city's commercial centres.
- **10:30: Yanaka Ginza:** A compact neighbourhood shopping street known for everyday food stalls, cat motifs and a lived-in local atmosphere. Try menchi katsu, a crisp breaded minced-meat croquette.
- **11:45** Take public transport to **Kagurazaka**.
- **12:30: Kagurazaka lunch:** This former geisha district has an unusual concentration of French-Japanese bistros alongside traditional Japanese restaurants. Stop for lunch here to try that mix of cuisines and break up the walk.
- **13:30: Kagurazaka lanes:** Hyogo Yokocho and Kakurenbo Yokocho are narrow stone-paved alleys with small shrines and surviving ryotei façades. Ryotei are traditional high-end dining houses. The lanes have a texture and atmosphere missing from the main shopping street.
- **14:30: Jimbocho:** Tokyo's specialist book district, packed with second-hand and antiquarian shops. Browse the second-hand shelves; larger stores also carry foreign-language sections.
- **15:30: Ladrio:** A traditional kissaten, or old-school Japanese coffee house, with mid-twentieth-century Showa atmosphere and Wiener coffee topped with cream. It is a place to rest and try Tokyo's retro café culture.
- **16:20** Continue to Akihabara.
- **16:30–17:30: Bic Camera AKIBA:** A seven-floor electronics department store covering computers, gaming, phones, audio, cameras, watches and toys. A focused hour gives you an efficient first look at Japanese electronics while leaving the more specialised Akihabara session for later.
- **17:30–18:15** Return to Akasaka and rest.
- **19:15: Easy Akasaka dinner:** Choose yakitori, an izakaya or Korean food. Akasaka has strong casual options, and keeping dinner local prevents an already full day turning into another cross-city excursion.

Keep this to Bic Camera and skip a second Akihabara shopping circuit. Ochanomizu remains covered on September 23.

## Day 3: Monday, Sep 21: Meiji Shrine and Ginza, Respect for the Aged Day

**Holiday:** Respect for the Aged Day.
Shrine history followed by indoor Ginza shopping.

- **07:30: Meiji Shrine:** Tokyo's major imperial-era Shinto shrine, reached through a broad forested approach. Arrive early to see its monumental ritual setting before the holiday crowds build.
- **Late morning onward: Ginza shopping:** Ginza is Tokyo's polished luxury and design district. GINZA SIX brings contemporary architecture and fashion, Ginza Mitsukoshi is a classic department store with an excellent basement food hall, and AOKI Ginza flagship is a practical stop for Japanese business-casual clothing. Together they show three distinct sides of modern Japanese retail without repeating the same shop format.
- **Evening: Akasaka or Roppongi:** Return to Akasaka for the easiest finish. If enough energy remains, Roppongi is a nearby restaurant, art and nightlife district, but there is no need to treat it as another sightseeing stop.

## Day 4: Tuesday, Sep 22: birthday, Silver Week holiday

**Holiday:** Statutory holiday between Respect for the Aged Day and the Autumnal Equinox.
Garden, tea, modern fashion districts and a special kaiseki dinner.

- **08:30** Birthday breakfast at the hotel or a café near Akasaka.
- **09:00–10:10: Hamarikyu Gardens:** A former Tokugawa shogun estate built around a seawater tidal pond, bridges and carefully composed landscape, with traditional garden design beneath modern towers. Check the official operating status before departure.
- **10:10–10:40: Nakajima no Ochaya:** A teahouse set over Hamarikyu's pond, serving matcha (powdered green tea) with wagashi (seasonal Japanese confectionery). This is an atmospheric pause in the garden, not a formal tea ceremony, and gives you time to sit with the landscape.
- **10:40–11:20** Walk to Shimbashi Station and take the Ginza Line directly to Omote-sando.
- **11:20–12:00: Omotesando:** A tree-lined fashion boulevard whose flagship stores form an open-air catalogue of contemporary architecture. Go for the flagship architecture and tree-lined streetscape; treat luxury shopping as secondary.
- **12:00–12:50: Omotesando or Harajuku lunch:** Choose a quick Japanese set meal, noodles or tonkatsu, a breaded pork cutlet that makes a substantial but time-efficient lunch. Eating here keeps the west-Tokyo route intact and avoids a separate food detour.
- **12:50–13:45: Takeshita Street and Harajuku side streets:** Takeshita is the concentrated, colourful face of youth and kawaii fashion, the Japanese cute-pop aesthetic; the side streets reveal smaller independent shops. This is the trip's concentrated look at Tokyo youth subculture, quite different from Ginza.
- **13:45–14:30: Cat Street:** A pedestrian-friendly backstreet linking Harajuku and Shibuya, lined with boutiques, vintage shops and streetwear. Browse the shops as you walk between the two districts.
- **14:30–16:15: Shibuya:** Tokyo's high-energy youth and entertainment centre. Start at Hachiko, the loyal-dog memorial statue that became the district's famous meeting point, then take in the scramble crossing and focused browsing. The crossing and surrounding shops give you a close look at Shibuya's crowds and retail culture.
- **16:15** Take the Ginza Line from Shibuya to Akasaka-mitsuke; reach the hotel around **16:45** for a proper rest, shower and change.
- **Approximately 18:15** Leave the hotel for Nishi-Azabu.
- **19:00: Jushu (壽修), BOOKED:** An intimate Kansai-style kaiseki restaurant where chef Masaki Senzaki builds a multi-course dinner around seafood, vegetables and Saga beef. The small room and ingredient-led cooking make it the birthday centrepiece. Address: 1F Saita Building, 2-16-1 Nishi-Azabu, Minato-ku, approximately nine minutes on foot from Omotesando Station. Smart casual; no shorts or sandals; avoid strong perfume; arrive at least five minutes early. Submit dietary restrictions in advance; day-of requests are not accepted. Fish/bonito/dashi and soy-related allergies require particular care.
- **After dinner: optional:** Finish after the kaiseki, or stay in **Nishi-Azabu**, an upmarket but low-key dining enclave around Jushu, for one quiet drink; use livelier **Roppongi** only if you want a late-night bar district. Return to Akasaka for the simplest finish.

Reconfirm Jushu’s exceptional public-holiday service through OMAKASE or the restaurant one to two weeks before travel.

## Day 5: Wednesday, Sep 23: explorer day, Autumnal Equinox Day

**Holiday:** Autumnal Equinox Day and the final day of Silver Week.

- **08:20** Leave Hotel Monterey Akasaka and walk to the Akasaka-mitsuke/Nagatacho station complex.
- Take the Yurakucho Line directly from **Nagatacho to Gokokuji**. Use Exit 6, then walk approximately ten minutes.
- **09:00–09:45: St Mary’s Cathedral, Tokyo:** Kenzo Tange's 1964 cathedral of curved concrete shells and stainless-steel cladding is one of the day's main works of modern architecture. See the exterior, detached bell tower and cross-lit main interior. Entry is conditional on there being no Mass or private event; keep quiet around worshippers.
- **09:45–10:30** Return to Gokokuji Station, take the Yurakucho Line to Iidabashi, change for Ochanomizu and walk to **Kanda Myojin**.
- **10:30–11:10: Kanda Myojin Shrine:** A long-established shrine associated with central Tokyo, local business and the nearby electronics district. It connects the day's older religious landscape to modern commercial Tokyo.
- **11:15–12:00: Ochanomizu and Nikolai Cathedral:** Ochanomizu is Tokyo's concentrated musical-instrument district, useful for browsing guitars and specialist shops. Nikolai Cathedral is a nineteenth-century Japanese Orthodox landmark whose domed exterior adds an unexpected architectural contrast; general tourist viewing inside is closed on national holidays.
- **12:00–13:00: Ochanomizu lunch:** Choose tonkatsu (breaded pork cutlet) for a substantial classic or soba (buckwheat noodles) for a lighter meal. Both are fast, dependable formats that fit the route without turning lunch into an event.
- **13:00–13:30** Continue to the Imperial Palace's **Kitahanebashi-mon Gate**.
- **13:30–15:00: Imperial Palace East Gardens:** The publicly accessible former Honmaru and Ninomaru compounds of Edo Castle, including stone ramparts, guardhouses, the old keep base and a Japanese garden. The castle remains explain the site's history. The residential palace is not open for independent visits, and the national-holiday guided tour does not operate. Enter through Kitahanebashi-mon and exit through Ote-mon towards Tokyo Station. Admission is free.
- **15:15–16:00: Tokyo Station and Marunouchi:** The restored red-brick station anchors Tokyo's formal business district. Stop for the architecture and urban design of KITTE's converted postal building and the ordered Marunouchi streetscape.
- **16:00–16:45** Travel to Kamiyacho and follow the underground signs to Azabudai Hills Garden Plaza B. Arrive before the booked window.
- **17:00–17:30 entry: teamLab Borderless, BOOKED:** A mapless immersive digital-art museum in which light-based works move and interact across rooms. Allow approximately two hours for a contemporary visual experience after the day's religious and imperial architecture. Keep the ticket QR accessible. Quantity, price and reservation reference were not supplied.
- **Approximately 19:00–19:15** Walk roughly 1 km from Azabudai Hills to Tokyo Tower.
- **19:15–19:45: Tokyo Tower exterior and Shiba Park:** Tokyo's 1958 red-and-white broadcasting tower is one of the defining images of post-war Tokyo. See it illuminated from the park and skip the observation deck because Shibuya Sky already provides the trip's panoramic ascent.
- **Evening:** Have dinner around Azabudai, Toranomon or Akasaka, then return to the hotel.

## Day 6: Thursday, Sep 24: classic Tokyo and shopping

Route: Asakusa, Ueno, Shibuya, Akihabara, then Okachimachi/Ueno. The day combines a focused museum visit, Shibuya Sky and shopping for games, electronics and clothing.

Keep the main case in Tokyo through the shopping day. At Hotel Monterey check-in on Sep 19, ask the front desk to confirm its Sep 25 Yamato cutoff and contact Mitsui Garden Hotel Kyoto Shinmachi Bettei to confirm receipt and holding. Pack the shopping into the main case that evening; hand it over at checkout on Sep 25 with Sep 26 as the preferred delivery date. Use a Sep 24 handover only if either hotel cannot confirm the Sep 25 plan.

- **08:00: Senso-ji:** Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple and the historic core of Asakusa, approached through monumental gates and a large incense-filled precinct. Arrive early for the Edo-Tokyo atmosphere before the shopping street becomes crowded.
- **Approximately 09:00–09:20: Nakamise-dori:** The traditional shop-lined approach between Senso-ji's outer and inner gates, selling snacks, crafts and souvenirs. A short pass covers the ceremonial approach and street texture; this is not a major shopping mission.
- **09:20: Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center:** Kengo Kuma's stacked, timber-screened visitor centre opposite Senso-ji. Its free observation level gives a compact aerial view over Nakamise, the temple roofs and the surrounding city.
- **Approximately 09:35** Travel to Ueno.
- **10:00–11:00: Tokyo National Museum:** Japan's principal museum of art and archaeology. A tightly focused hour in the Honkan Japanese Gallery covers sculpture, screens, ceramics and samurai material without exhausting the day in the enormous collection. General collection admission is currently ¥1,000; buy the flexible collection ticket online beforehand to avoid possible ticket-counter queues.
- **11:15–11:40: Quick Ueno or Ameyoko lunch:** Ameyoko is a noisy former black-market lane now packed with food stalls and bargain shops. Use its station-facing end for noodles, rice or a snack because speed matters before the fixed Shibuya Sky entry; the full market is not the point today.
- **Approximately 11:45** Take the JR Yamanote Line directly from Ueno to Shibuya. Allow time to navigate Shibuya Station and reach the 14th-floor entrance before the booked window.
- **12:40–12:59: Shibuya Sky, BOOKED:** A high-level indoor gallery and open-air rooftop above Shibuya Scramble Square. The broad view over Tokyo and the crossing below justify the cross-city loop. Enter during this window and allow approximately 80–90 minutes, subject to live rooftop operation.
- **Approximately 14:20** Leave Shibuya for Akihabara on the JR Yamanote Line.
- **15:00–16:30: Akihabara:** Tokyo's dense electronics, gaming and anime retail district. Prioritise Yodobashi Akiba, a vast all-purpose electronics store, plus only one specialist anchor: Super Potato for retro games, GiGO or TAITO Station for playing arcade games, or Mandarake Complex for second-hand games, manga, figures and collectables. The one-anchor rule preserves energy and prevents repetitive browsing.
- **Approximately 16:35** Travel one stop to Okachimachi.
- **16:45–17:15: mita sneakers:** An independent specialist trainer shop known for collaborations and Japan-market releases. Its selection differs from the global chains, so keep this a short targeted visit.
- **17:20–18:20: HINOYA Main Store:** A long-running Ameyoko retailer specialising in Japanese denim, military-inspired clothing and heritage casualwear. This is the priority fitting block because Japanese-made denim is difficult to judge properly without trying it on.
- **18:25–18:55: GU Okachimachi:** Uniqlo's lower-priced sister brand, focused on trend-led jeans, casualwear and T-shirts. Make this a quick value check after the specialist shops, and leave promptly if the current range does not appeal.
- **19:00: Decision point:** @cosme STORE Ueno Marui is a large beauty retailer useful for comparing Japanese skincare and cosmetics, while JINS atré Ueno offers fast, practical Japanese eyewear. Use one only if it still solves a shopping need and is open; otherwise go directly to dinner.
- **Evening:** eat in the selected shopping district or return to Akasaka for an easy final Tokyo dinner. Keep this flexible and do not add another reservation.

**Tsukiji standby:** The outer market is a dense cluster of seafood, produce and specialist food shops that survived the wholesale market's move to Toyosu. Consider it for its food-market energy only as a substitute for either Asakusa or the museum-and-Ameyoko section. Do not add it as another stop.

## Day 8: Saturday, Sep 26: Shuzenji Onsen slow day

Temple history, river-town atmosphere and time to enjoy the ryokan.

- **Morning: Kikuya breakfast:** The included ryokan breakfast is a composed Japanese meal and part of the stay experience, with no reason to rush out.
- **10:00: Shuzenji Temple and Hie Shrine:** The Buddhist temple that gave the town its name and its neighbouring Shinto shrine form Shuzenji's historic and spiritual core. Start here because the onsen settlement grew around them.
- **11:30: Tokko-no-Yu:** A small riverside pavilion marking Shuzenji's legendary hot-spring origin. Stop briefly for the context. It is a landmark only; bathing and foot bathing are not permitted.
- **12:00: Bamboo Forest Path, Shigetsuden and Yoriie's grave:** A short riverside bamboo walk leads to a small repository and memorials tied to the exiled shogun Minamoto no Yoriie. Together they add atmosphere and Kamakura-period history without turning the slow day into a long hike.
- **13:00: Local onsen-town lunch:** Choose soba (buckwheat noodles), a simple set meal or a café lunch. Keep it flexible so the day stays centred on Shuzenji and the ryokan instead of a distant Izu excursion.
- **14:30: Kikuya baths and rest:** Kikuya is a historic ryokan, or traditional Japanese inn, arranged through linked corridors, gardens and bath areas. Give the return block plenty of time because the baths and building are central to the stay, alongside the room.
- **17:30 or 19:45: Included Kikuya dinner:** A multi-course ryokan meal served as part of the half-board stay. The meal is part of the ryokan experience, so choose the seating at check-in and skip a separate restaurant hunt.
- **After dinner: optional illuminated Bamboo Forest Path:** A quiet repeat of the short bamboo walk after dark, if you want a gentle post-dinner stroll. The official tourism association lists lighting until 23:00.

Travel day and first Kyoto evening.

- **Morning** Included final Kikuya breakfast; one last private bath
- **10:00** Check out comfortably before the 11:00 deadline. Tokai Bus to Shuzenji Station (~8 min), then the Izu-Hakone Railway to Mishima (~30 min)
- **11:46–13:37: SmartEX confirmed:** **Hikari 709, Mishima to Kyoto**, Green Car 10, seats **4-C and 4-D**, **¥29,540 total for two**.
- **~14:00: Kyoto Station lunch if needed:** Use an ekiben (a regional boxed railway meal), noodle counter or station food hall. A quick lunch leaves the afternoon free for Kyoto.
- **~15:00** Check into **Mitsui Garden Hotel Kyoto Shinmachi Bettei** (361 Rokkaku-cho, Shinmachi-dori, Karasuma side, 7 min from Shijo station)

Use it as a short introduction to local food culture. Individual stalls vary and many close early on Sundays, so do not rely on it as a full grazing session.
- **17:00: Pontocho Alley:** A narrow lantern-lined restaurant alley running parallel to the Kamo River. Scout it for its compressed old-Kyoto atmosphere and practical cluster of dinner options.

Kiyamachi is the livelier canal-side restaurant street parallel to Pontocho, giving more informal choice when the tiny alley is busy. Treat any geiko (traditional performing artist) or apprentice maiko sighting as incidental, never something to chase or photograph intrusively.

## Day 10: Monday, Sep 28: east Kyoto temples and traditions

Eastern Kyoto temple circuit.

- **06:00: Fushimi Inari Taisha at dawn:** The head shrine of Inari, the Shinto deity associated with rice and prosperity, famous for thousands of donated vermilion torii shrine gates climbing Mount Inari. The early start puts you in the gate tunnels before tour groups arrive. Hike only to the Yotsutsuji intersection for city views; the Senbon Torii double rows appear near the beginning, so there is no need to summit.
- **08:00** Breakfast near Fushimi Inari or grab coffee on the way to the next stop
- **09:30: Kiyomizu-dera, Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka:** Kiyomizu-dera is a major hillside Buddhist temple whose wooden stage opens over eastern Kyoto; the preserved sloping lanes below retain traditional shopfronts and townscape. Together, they combine temple architecture, city views and historic-street atmosphere in one stop.
- **11:00: Kodai-ji or Yasaka Shrine:** Kodai-ji is a refined Zen memorial temple with gardens and links to Toyotomi Hideyoshi's widow; Yasaka is Gion's lively guardian shrine. Choose Kodai-ji for designed tranquillity or Yasaka for a shorter, more energetic stop. Keeping this as a choice prevents temple overload.
- **12:00: Yudofu lunch in Gion:** Yudofu is gently simmered tofu served with simple accompaniments, a Kyoto temple-cuisine speciality. Gion is the historic geiko district at the centre of this Higashiyama route, making it the natural place for a distinctly local, light lunch between temple stops.
- **13:30: Philosopher's Path and Ginkaku-ji:** The Philosopher's Path is a two-kilometre canal walk linking quiet residential north-east Kyoto to Ginkaku-ji. Visit the "Silver Pavilion" for its carefully composed dry garden, moss and villa landscape, a calmer stop after busy Kiyomizu.
- **15:30: Nanzen-ji:** A major Zen temple complex with broad precincts and a striking nineteenth-century brick aqueduct crossing the grounds. The grounds pair a Zen landscape with nineteenth-century industrial engineering; the aqueduct area is free to visit.
- **17:00** Return to hotel, rest
- **18:30: Mid-range kaiseki dinner in Gion:** Kaiseki is a seasonal multi-course format balancing flavour, technique, tableware and presentation. A mid-range version shows the city's formal cuisine without repeating the birthday dinner's full splurge.

## Day 11: Tuesday, Sep 29: Uji and Tōfuku-ji

Heian-era architecture, the oldest extant Shinto shrine, Uji tea culture and a modernist Zen garden.

Uji adds the trip's only Heian-era architecture (Byōdō-in, 1053), the oldest extant Shinto shrine (Ujigami, UNESCO) and tea culture. The JR Nara line connects it to Mirei Shigemori's 1939 modernist Zen garden at Tōfuku-ji. This leaves the forest and mountain temples for Wednesday's Kurama-Kibune walk.

- **10:00** Hotel departure. Walk to Kyoto Station. JR Nara line to **Uji** (~30 min local, ¥240, or ~17 min rapid)
- **11:00: Byōdō-in Phoenix Hall:** An eleventh-century aristocratic Pure Land Buddhist temple whose symmetrical hall and reflecting pond appear on the ¥10 coin. It is the trip's clearest surviving Heian-era architecture. The grounds ticket includes the **Hōshō-kan**, an underground-designed museum displaying the temple bell, phoenix ornaments, statues of Buddhist figures riding clouds and other original treasures. These details cannot be read from the hall's exterior. Grounds and museum ¥700; the hall interior tour is another ¥300 via limited same-day timed slots, so queue early. Allow about 75 minutes.
- **12:30: Uji tea lunch:** Uji is a Japanese tea centre. Choose Tsuen Tea, a centuries-old bridge-side teahouse, for its long history and riverside setting; Nakamura Tokichi for matcha-infused soba and desserts, accepting longer queues; or Fukujuen Uji Workshop to roast hōjicha, a nutty roasted green tea, yourself. Lunch here gives you time to taste Uji tea and try dishes made with it. Budget ¥1,500–2,500 per person.
- **13:45: Ujigami Jinja:** A small UNESCO-listed shrine preserving Japan's oldest extant Shinto shrine buildings. Its quiet scale and ancient timber architecture are markedly different from Fushimi Inari's monumental gate landscape. Free; allow about 30 minutes.
- **14:30: Uji River walk:** A calm riverside route with Tale of Genji literary connections. It connects the day's main sights without dead transit time and leaves room for an optional second tea tasting.
- **15:15** JR Nara line back toward Kyoto, alight at **Tōfuku-ji** (~15 min)
- **Approximately 15:40: Tōfuku-ji:** One of Kyoto's Five Great Zen temples, included here for Mirei Shigemori's 1939 Hōjō Garden, where chequerboard moss and stone recast the Zen garden in modernist form. It brings twentieth-century design into a day of ancient architecture. Hōjō garden ¥500. Entry closes at **16:00**, leaving roughly 50 minutes before the 16:30 closing; if the Uji morning slips, drop it.
- **17:00** Train back to Kyoto centre
- **~19:00: Light dinner:** Choose **Pontocho**, the narrow lantern-lit restaurant alley by the Kamo River, for obanzai, seasonal Kyoto home cooking served as small dishes. The **Nishiki** food-market area is the simpler alternative. Both sit conveniently in central Kyoto, and the birthday dinner and two ryokan dinners already cover the trip's high-end meals.

**Tōfuku-ji timing:** Tōfuku-ji entry closes at 16:00, leaving a short focused visit if the Uji morning stays efficient. If the route slips, drop Tōfuku-ji; Uji alone is still a complete day. All stops are walk-up, with no advance bookings needed.

## Day 12: Wednesday, Sep 30: Kurama to Kibune mountain day

Ancient cedar forest, mountain-temple history and Kifune Shrine.

- **10:00** Walk or take the Keihan line to Demachiyanagi, then the **Eizan Railway** to Kurama (~30 min, ¥470 pp)
- **10:50** Arrive **Kurama**. Optional cable car ¥200 to skip the first steep stretch, or walk the full path
- **11:00: Yuki-jinja:** A small mountainside shrine reached through an old cedar avenue on the climb from Kurama. Pause here because the approach establishes the forest's sacred character before the main temple.
- **11:30: Kurama-dera:** A mountain temple founded in the eighth century and wrapped in legends of tengu spirits and warrior training. Its appeal lies in the pilgrimage atmosphere and deep-forest setting as much as the buildings.
- **12:15: Kurama-to-Kibune trail:** A roughly one-hour forest crossing over the mountain, with mossy roots and ancient cedars. The hike is the day's central experience because it turns two temple villages into one coherent journey.
- **13:15: Kifune Shrine:** A water deity shrine climbing the valley in lantern-lined stone terraces, with distinctive water-revealed fortunes. It is the natural end of the mountain crossing and adds a different, river-focused form of Shinto landscape.
- **13:45: Kibune lunch:** Take a flexible late meal in the narrow riverside village to recover after the trail. Verify a suitable current restaurant and hours during the final pre-trip check; do not lock in an unverified venue.
- **15:30** Walk to Kibuneguchi, then take the Eizan Railway back towards Demachiyanagi (~30 min)
- **16:00: Shimogamo Shrine:** One of Kyoto's oldest Shinto complexes, set within the ancient Tadasu no Mori woodland. It is directly on the return route and provides formal shrine architecture in a flat, spacious forest after the rugged mountain sites.
- **17:00** Back to hotel area, rest
- **~19:00: Light dinner:** Choose a depachika bento, a polished boxed meal from a department-store basement food hall, or soba, buckwheat noodles served hot or cold, near the hotel. Both are regional, efficient recovery food after the hike, with no second evening programme needed.

**Weather contingency:** The valley, temple and hike can work in light rain. In heavy rain, switch to **Daitoku-ji's sub-temples**, a compact cluster of Zen gardens and historic temple architecture, then **Walden Woods**, a minimalist speciality-coffee room for a break indoors, followed by **Pontocho**, Kyoto's narrow lantern-lit riverside restaurant alley. This keeps the day's architecture and atmosphere without an exposed mountain trail. Decide morning-of from the JMA forecast.

## Day 13: Thursday, Oct 1: Kyoto to Nara to Osaka

Kyoto checkout, Nara's giant Buddha and deer, then Osaka.

- **10:00** Check out of the Kyoto hotel with the small travelling bag. The main suitcase should already be with Yamato en route to Haneda Terminal 3, having been handed over on Sep 30, or Sep 29 if the route-specific cutoff required it
- **10:30** Train to **Nara** (~45 min, ¥720 by JR or Kintetsu)
- **11:30: Nara Park:** A broad historic park where free-roaming deer are treated as sacred messengers. The deer provide atmosphere and connect the major temple sites, but keep food away except when deliberately offering official crackers.
- **12:00: Tōdai-ji:** A monumental Buddhist temple whose Great Buddha Hall shelters one of Japan's largest bronze Buddha images. Its scale and the political-religious ambition of eighth-century Japan are the main reasons for the Nara stop.
- **13:00: Nara lunch:** Try kakinoha-zushi, pressed sushi wrapped in persimmon leaf for preservation, or use Higashimuki's covered shopping street for a quick set meal. The regional sushi is the more distinctive choice; the arcade is the efficient weatherproof fallback.
- **14:00: Kasuga Taisha:** Nara's great vermilion Shinto shrine, approached through a forest of donated stone lanterns and filled with bronze lanterns inside. It is a more intimate, wooded counterpart to Tōdai-ji's monumental Buddhism.
- **15:30** Train from Nara to **Osaka Namba** (~40 min, ¥570)
- **16:00** Check into **Via Inn Prime Shinsaibashi Yotsubashi** (1-5-10 Shinmachi, Nishi-ku, Yotsubashi station, 1 min). From Osaka-Namba, take the Yotsubashi line 1 stop to Yotsubashi, or walk west for 12 min. Drop bags. *(AEON supermarket on the ground floor for snacks/water.)*
- **17:00: Dotonbori and takoyaki:** Dotonbori is Osaka's neon canal-side entertainment strip, centred on animated signs and the Glico Running Man, the illuminated running-athlete billboard used as a meeting point and visual emblem. Walk along the canal to see the illuminated signs on your first evening. Try takoyaki, soft-centred octopus batter balls, at established specialists Wanaka or Kogaryu. Then pass through Shinsaibashi-suji, the long covered retail arcade linking Dotonbori to the hotel side of central Osaka; it adds local shopping bustle and completes the route.
- **19:00: Okonomiyaki dinner:** Osaka-style okonomiyaki is a savoury cabbage pancake cooked on a griddle with meat or seafood, sauce and bonito flakes. Try this Osaka staple at Fukutaro for the convenient Namba location, or Michelin-listed Mizuno, the long-established Dotonbori restaurant, accepting its usual queue.
- **20:30: Dotonbori after dark:** Return to the canal once the signs are fully lit. If you are still hungry, try kushikatsu, breaded, deep-fried skewers, at a standing bar.

## Day 14: Friday, Oct 2: Osaka neighbourhoods

Street food and local neighbourhoods.

- **09:30: Kuromon Market:** A covered food market historically known as "Osaka's kitchen", now increasingly tourist-oriented and expensive. Give it a quick atmospheric walk-through, then save appetite and money for Shinsekai, the day's food destination.
- **10:30: Shinsekai:** A proudly retro entertainment district beneath Tsutenkaku Tower, mixing old signboards, cheap eateries and working-class Osaka character. It is rougher and more local than polished Dotonbori.
  - **Kushikatsu at Daruma:** Breaded skewers of meat, seafood and vegetables, fried to order. This is Shinsekai's signature food and Daruma is closely associated with the style; follow the current sauce instructions because the old communal-pot system may no longer apply.
  - **Janjan Yokocho:** A narrow covered alley of small eateries, game parlours and bars. Walk through for its retro atmosphere.
- **12:00: Shinsekai lunch:** Continue with kushikatsu if sharing a broad selection, or switch to ramen, wheat noodles in a savoury broth, at a local shop. Staying in the district keeps the route efficient and lets the neighbourhood drive the meal without a reservation.
- **13:30: Osaka Castle:** A modern reconstruction housing a museum within the vast stone walls and moats of the Toyotomi-era fortress site. Visit the grounds and exterior for their scale and political history; treat the interior museum as optional if energy is fading.
- **15:00: Nakazakicho:** A low-rise pre-war neighbourhood repurposed with independent cafés, vintage shops and small creative businesses. Its intimate scale and adaptive reuse differ from Osaka's big commercial districts.
- **16:30: Tenjinbashi-suji:** A very long covered shopping arcade serving everyday local life. Browse, snack and people-watch to see the neighbourhood's everyday shopping streets.
- **18:00** Back to Namba
- **19:00: Tachinomi crawl:** Tachinomi are compact standing bars built around inexpensive drinks and small plates such as karaage (Japanese fried chicken), grilled fish and whisky-and-soda highballs. Two or three near Namba make a fitting final dinner because the format is social, flexible and distinctly urban Osaka without requiring another long sit-down meal.
- **21:00: Optional farewell Dotonbori walk:** Revisit the neon canal after the standing-bar dinner only if one last burst of Osaka energy appeals.

## Day 15: Saturday, Oct 3: Osaka to Haneda and departure

Direct Osaka-to-airport departure day.

- **10:00** Check out from Via Inn Prime with the small travelling bag.
- **~10:15** Walk ~7 min to **Shinsaibashi**, then take the **Midōsuji Line direct to Shin-Osaka** (~15 min, no transfer).
- **11:15–13:38: SmartEX confirmed:** **Nozomi 126, Shin-Osaka to Shinagawa**, ordinary Car 4, seats **19-D and 19-E**, **¥29,040 total for two**. The main suitcase should be waiting at Haneda; if the fallback leaves a 161–250cm case travelling with you, change to the required oversized-baggage seat type, subject to availability.
- **~13:45** Transfer at Shinagawa to the Keikyu Line for **Haneda Airport Terminal 3**.
- **~16:15: Haneda Terminal 3:** Collect the main suitcase from the **Yamato receipt counter, 2F Arrival Lobby**, using the sender receipt, then take it to Air China check-in. A final konbini (Japanese convenience-store) haul is useful for snacks and practical flight food without risking a slow restaurant before departure.
- **19:15** Depart **CA168 HND to PEK** (Air China, T3), then CA851 PEK 02:50 to LGW 06:35 (Oct 4)
  - Confirm CA168's exact departure with the airline: your booking shows 19:20, while schedule databases show 19:15. Either leaves enough buffer, but confirm the time before planning check-in.

## JR Pass verdict: skip it

Individual intercity tickets are about **¥34,000 per person**. A 7-day JR Pass cannot cover both the Sep 25 Izu leg and the Oct 3 Osaka-to-Tokyo departure leg inside one validity window, and it would not cover the Izu-Hakone Railway or Tokai Bus. Buy individual tickets.
