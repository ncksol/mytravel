## Day 1 — Saturday, Sep 19: Arrival

**Theme:** Arrive, settle into Akasaka and keep the first evening pressure-free.

- **14:30** Arrive at Tokyo Haneda Airport.
- **Approximately 15:30–16:30** Complete immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Allow up to two hours before becoming concerned.
- At the airport, activate the pre-arranged eSIM or collect pocket Wi-Fi, set up Mobile Suica or obtain an available physical IC card, and withdraw approximately **¥20,000–30,000** as initial cash.
- **Immediately after clearing the airport** Travel to Akasaka by Keikyu/Metro or monorail/Metro. Allow 45–60 minutes and approximately ¥700.
- **Likely approximately 16:15–17:45** Check into Hotel Monterey Akasaka, freshen up and assess energy honestly.
- **18:30 — Izakaya dinner near the hotel:** An izakaya is a relaxed Japanese pub built around drinks and shareable dishes; try yakitori (grilled chicken skewers), seasonal small plates and draft beer. It is the best first-night choice because ordering can scale to your appetite and Akasaka avoids unnecessary travel after the flight.
- **18:30 — Optional high-energy alternative, Omoide Yokocho:** A maze of tiny, post-war-style yakitori counters beside Shinjuku Station. It earns its place for smoky, close-quarters old-Tokyo atmosphere; try negima (chicken and spring onion), tsukune (chicken meatball) and kawa (chicken skin).
- **20:00 — Optional, Golden Gai:** Several lanes of minute, highly individual bars in Shinjuku. Go for the unusual micro-bar atmosphere rather than bargain drinking; choose a clearly foreigner-friendly bar and expect a possible ¥500–1,000 cover charge.

Akasaka is the recommended plan if jet lag is noticeable. Omoide Yokocho will be particularly busy on the opening Saturday of Silver Week, and Golden Gai remains entirely optional.

## Day 2 — Sunday, Sep 20: Hidden Tokyo Neighbourhoods 🔴 Silver Week

**Theme:** Older residential Tokyo, shrine lanes, bookshops, retro cafés and a focused electronics-store visit. This is a full day.

- **08:30 — Quick breakfast:** Pick up onigiri (filled rice triangles), fruit and coffee from a konbini, one of Japan's exceptionally well-stocked convenience stores. This is the efficient local option before a full walking day.
- **09:00 — Nezu Shrine:** A historic Shinto shrine complex with wooded grounds and a small tunnel of vermilion torii gates. It earns its place as a calm, traditional opening to the older neighbourhoods of north-east Tokyo.
- **10:00 — Yanaka:** A low-rise district of temples, wooden houses and quiet lanes that survived much of Tokyo's twentieth-century destruction. Walking there shows a residential, slower-paced counterpoint to the city's commercial centres.
- **10:30 — Yanaka Ginza:** A compact neighbourhood shopping street known for everyday food stalls and cat motifs. It is worth including for lived-in local atmosphere; try menchi katsu, a crisp breaded minced-meat croquette.
- **11:45** Take public transport to **Kagurazaka**.
- **12:30 — Kagurazaka lunch:** This former geisha district has an unusual concentration of French-Japanese bistros alongside traditional Japanese restaurants. Lunch belongs here because the food reflects the neighbourhood's layered cultural identity and breaks the route naturally.
- **13:30 — Kagurazaka lanes:** Hyogo Yokocho and Kakurenbo Yokocho are narrow stone-paved alleys with small shrines and surviving ryotei—traditional high-end dining-house—façades. They earn the time for texture and atmosphere that the main shopping street does not provide.
- **14:30 — Jimbocho:** Tokyo's specialist book district, packed with second-hand and antiquarian shops. It is on the list for browsing culture rather than a single sight; larger stores also carry foreign-language sections.
- **15:30 — Ladrio:** A traditional kissaten—an old-school Japanese coffee house—with mid-twentieth-century Showa atmosphere and Wiener coffee topped with cream. The stop provides both a rest and a taste of Tokyo's retro café culture.
- **16:20** Continue to Akihabara.
- **16:30–17:30 — Bic Camera AKIBA:** A seven-floor electronics department store covering computers, gaming, phones, audio, cameras, watches and toys. It earns a focused hour as an efficient first look at Japanese electronics without consuming the later, more specialised Akihabara session.
- **17:30–18:15** Return to Akasaka and rest.
- **19:15 — Easy Akasaka dinner:** Choose yakitori, an izakaya or Korean food. Akasaka has strong casual options, and keeping dinner local prevents an already full day turning into another cross-city excursion.

Keep this to Bic Camera rather than extending into a second Akihabara shopping circuit. Ochanomizu remains covered on September 23.

## Day 3 — Monday, Sep 21: Meiji Shrine and Ginza 🔴 Respect for the Aged Day

**Holiday:** Respect for the Aged Day.
**Theme:** Shrine history followed by indoor Ginza shopping.

- **07:30 — Meiji Shrine:** Tokyo's major imperial-era Shinto shrine, reached through a broad forested approach. The early visit earns its place for the contrast between monumental shrine ritual and the city outside, before holiday crowds build.
- **Late morning onward — Ginza shopping:** Ginza is Tokyo's polished luxury and design district. **GINZA SIX** brings contemporary architecture and fashion, **Ginza Mitsukoshi** is a classic department store with an excellent basement food hall, and **AOKI Ginza flagship** is a practical stop for Japanese business-casual clothing. Together they show three distinct sides of modern Japanese retail rather than repeating the same shop format.
- **Evening — Akasaka or Roppongi:** Return to Akasaka for the easiest finish, or use Roppongi—a nearby restaurant, art and nightlife district—only if enough energy remains. It is an optional convenience, not another sightseeing obligation.

## Day 4 — Tuesday, Sep 22: 🎂 Birthday 🔴 Silver Week Holiday

**Holiday:** Statutory holiday between Respect for the Aged Day and the Autumnal Equinox.
**Theme:** Garden, tea, modern fashion districts and a special kaiseki dinner.

- **08:30** Birthday breakfast at the hotel or a café near Akasaka.
- **09:00–10:10 — Hamarikyu Gardens:** A former Tokugawa shogun estate built around a seawater tidal pond, bridges and carefully composed landscape. It earns its place for feudal history and the striking contrast of traditional garden design beneath modern towers. Check the official operating status before departure.
- **10:10–10:40 — Nakajima no Ochaya:** A teahouse set over Hamarikyu's pond, serving matcha (powdered green tea) with wagashi (seasonal Japanese confectionery). This is an atmospheric garden pause—not a formal tea ceremony—and makes the landscape more than a walk-through sight.
- **10:40–11:20** Walk to Shimbashi Station and take the Ginza Line directly to Omote-sando.
- **11:20–12:00 — Omotesando:** A tree-lined fashion boulevard whose flagship stores form an open-air catalogue of contemporary architecture. It earns its place for design and streetscape, not merely luxury shopping.
- **12:00–12:50 — Omotesando or Harajuku lunch:** Choose a quick Japanese set meal, noodles or tonkatsu—the breaded pork cutlet that makes a substantial but time-efficient lunch. Eating here protects the west-Tokyo route and avoids a separate food detour.
- **12:50–13:45 — Takeshita Street and Harajuku side streets:** Takeshita is the concentrated, colourful face of youth and kawaii—the Japanese cute-pop aesthetic—fashion; the side streets reveal smaller independent shops. It is worth seeing for a subculture-driven Tokyo that sharply contrasts with Ginza.
- **13:45–14:30 — Cat Street:** A pedestrian-friendly backstreet linking Harajuku and Shibuya, lined with boutiques, vintage shops and streetwear. It earns its place as both an interesting retail district and the most atmospheric way to walk between the two hubs.
- **14:30–16:15 — Shibuya:** Tokyo's high-energy youth and entertainment centre. Start at **Hachiko**, the loyal-dog memorial statue that became the district's famous meeting point, then take in the scramble crossing and focused browsing. Together they form a compact experience of the city's scale, movement and commercial spectacle.
- **16:15** Take the Ginza Line from Shibuya to Akasaka-mitsuke; reach the hotel around **16:45** for a proper rest, shower and change.
- **Approximately 18:15** Leave the hotel for Nishi-Azabu.
- **19:00 — Jushu (壽修) ✅ BOOKED:** An intimate Kansai-style kaiseki restaurant where chef Masaki Senzaki builds a multi-course dinner around seafood, vegetables and Saga beef. It is the birthday centrepiece because the small room and ingredient-led cooking provide one genuinely special meal rather than generic luxury. Address: 1F Saita Building, 2-16-1 Nishi-Azabu, Minato-ku, approximately nine minutes on foot from Omotesando Station. Smart casual; no shorts or sandals; avoid strong perfume; arrive at least five minutes early. Submit dietary restrictions in advance; day-of requests are not accepted. Fish/bonito/dashi and soy-related allergies require particular care.
- **After dinner — optional:** Finish after the kaiseki, or stay in **Nishi-Azabu**, an upmarket but low-key dining enclave around Jushu, for one quiet drink; use livelier **Roppongi** only if you want a late-night bar district. Return to Akasaka for the simplest finish. This remains optional because Jushu is the evening's main experience.

Reconfirm Jushu’s exceptional public-holiday service through OMAKASE or the restaurant one to two weeks before travel.

## Day 5 — Wednesday, Sep 23: Explorer Day 🔴 Autumnal Equinox Day

**Holiday:** Autumnal Equinox Day and the final day of Silver Week.

#### Plan — St Mary’s Cathedral, Kanda, the Imperial Palace, teamLab and Tokyo Tower

- **08:20** Leave Hotel Monterey Akasaka and walk to the Akasaka-mitsuke/Nagatacho station complex.
- Take the Yurakucho Line directly from **Nagatacho to Gokokuji**. Use Exit 6, then walk approximately ten minutes.
- **09:00–09:45 — St Mary’s Cathedral, Tokyo:** Kenzo Tange's 1964 cathedral of curved concrete shells and stainless-steel cladding. It earns its place as one of Tokyo's strongest works of modern architecture; see the exterior, detached bell tower and cross-lit main interior. Entry is conditional on there being no Mass or private event; keep quiet around worshippers.
- **09:45–10:30** Return to Gokokuji Station, take the Yurakucho Line to Iidabashi, change for Ochanomizu and walk to **Kanda Myojin**.
- **10:30–11:10 — Kanda Myojin Shrine:** A long-established shrine associated with central Tokyo, local business and the nearby electronics district. It connects the day's older religious landscape to modern commercial Tokyo.
- **11:15–12:00 — Ochanomizu and Nikolai Cathedral:** Ochanomizu is Tokyo's concentrated musical-instrument district, useful for browsing guitars and specialist shops. Nikolai Cathedral is a nineteenth-century Japanese Orthodox landmark whose domed exterior adds an unexpected architectural contrast; general tourist viewing inside is closed on national holidays.
- **12:00–13:00 — Ochanomizu lunch:** Choose tonkatsu (breaded pork cutlet) for a substantial classic or soba (buckwheat noodles) for a lighter meal. Both are fast, dependable formats that fit the route without turning lunch into an event.
- **13:00–13:30** Continue to the Imperial Palace's **Kitahanebashi-mon Gate**.
- **13:30–15:00 — Imperial Palace East Gardens:** The publicly accessible former Honmaru and Ninomaru compounds of Edo Castle, including stone ramparts, guardhouses, the old keep base and a Japanese garden. This supplies the palace's real historical substance even though the residential palace is not open for independent visits; the national-holiday guided tour does not operate. Enter through Kitahanebashi-mon and exit through Ote-mon towards Tokyo Station. Admission is free.
- **15:15–16:00 — Tokyo Station and Marunouchi:** The restored red-brick station anchors Tokyo's formal business district. KITTE's converted postal building and the ordered Marunouchi streetscape earn the stop for architecture and urban design rather than another shopping session.
- **16:00–16:45** Travel to Kamiyacho and follow the underground signs to Azabudai Hills Garden Plaza B. Arrive before the booked window.
- **17:00–17:30 entry — teamLab Borderless ✅ BOOKED:** A mapless immersive digital-art museum in which light-based works move and interact across rooms. Allow approximately two hours; it earns its place as Tokyo's strongest contemporary visual experience and contrasts with the day's religious and imperial architecture. Keep the ticket QR accessible. Quantity, price and reservation reference were not supplied.
- **Approximately 19:00–19:15** Walk roughly 1 km from Azabudai Hills to Tokyo Tower.
- **19:15–19:45 — Tokyo Tower exterior and Shiba Park:** Tokyo's 1958 red-and-white broadcasting tower is one of the defining images of post-war Tokyo. See it illuminated from the park rather than paying for another observation deck; Shibuya Sky already provides the trip's panoramic ascent.
- **Evening:** Have dinner around Azabudai, Toranomon or Akasaka, then return to the hotel.

**Theme:** Asakusa, a focused museum visit, Shibuya Sky, games and electronics in Akihabara, then Ueno/Okachimachi shopping. Route: **Asakusa → Ueno → Shibuya → Akihabara → Okachimachi/Ueno**.

Pack the shopping into the main case that evening; hand it over at checkout on Sep 25 with Sep 26 as the preferred delivery date. - **08:00 — Senso-ji:** Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple and the historic core of Asakusa, approached through monumental gates and a large incense-filled precinct. It earns an early start for classic Edo-Tokyo atmosphere before the shopping street becomes crowded.
- **Approximately 09:00–09:20 — Nakamise-dori:** The traditional shop-lined approach between Senso-ji's outer and inner gates, selling snacks, crafts and souvenirs. It is worth a short pass for the ceremonial approach and street texture, not as a major shopping mission.
- **09:20 — Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center:** Kengo Kuma's stacked, timber-screened visitor centre opposite Senso-ji. Its free observation level earns the stop for a compact aerial view over Nakamise, the temple roofs and the surrounding city.
- **Approximately 09:35** Travel to Ueno.
- **10:00–11:00 — Tokyo National Museum:** Japan's principal museum of art and archaeology. A tightly focused Honkan Japanese Gallery visit earns its hour by giving historical context—sculpture, screens, ceramics and samurai material—without exhausting the day in the enormous collection. General collection admission is currently ¥1,000; buy the flexible collection ticket online beforehand to avoid possible ticket-counter queues.
- **11:15–11:40 — Quick Ueno or Ameyoko lunch:** Ameyoko is a noisy former black-market lane now packed with food stalls and bargain shops. Use its station-facing end for noodles, rice or a snack because speed matters before the fixed Shibuya Sky entry; the full market is not the point today.
- **Approximately 11:45** Take the JR Yamanote Line directly from Ueno to Shibuya. Allow time to navigate Shibuya Station and reach the 14th-floor entrance before the booked window.
- **12:40–12:59 — Shibuya Sky ✅ BOOKED:** A high-level indoor gallery and open-air rooftop above Shibuya Scramble Square. It earns the cross-city loop for a broad view over Tokyo and a different perspective on the crossing below; enter during this window and allow approximately 80–90 minutes, subject to live rooftop operation.
- **Approximately 14:20** Leave Shibuya for Akihabara on the JR Yamanote Line.
- **15:00–16:30 — Akihabara:** Tokyo's dense electronics, gaming and anime retail district. Prioritise **Yodobashi Akiba**, a vast all-purpose electronics store, plus only one specialist anchor: **Super Potato** for retro games, **GiGO** or **TAITO Station** for playing arcade games, or **Mandarake Complex** for second-hand games, manga, figures and collectables. - **17:20–18:20 — HINOYA Main Store:** A long-running Ameyoko retailer specialising in Japanese denim, military-inspired clothing and heritage casualwear. This is the priority fitting block because Japanese-made denim is difficult to judge properly without trying it on.
- **18:25–18:55 — GU Okachimachi:** Uniqlo's lower-priced sister brand, focused on trend-led jeans, casualwear and T-shirts. It earns a quick value check after the specialist shops; leave promptly if the current range does not appeal.
- **19:00 — Decision point:** **@cosme STORE Ueno Marui** is a large beauty retailer useful for comparing Japanese skincare and cosmetics, while **JINS atré Ueno** offers fast, practical Japanese eyewear. Use one only if it still solves a shopping need and is open; otherwise go directly to dinner.
- **Evening:** eat in the selected shopping district or return to Akasaka for an easy final Tokyo dinner. Keep this flexible rather than adding another reservation.

**Tsukiji standby:** The outer market is a dense cluster of seafood, produce and specialist food shops that survived the wholesale market's move to Toyosu. ## Day 8 — Saturday, Sep 26: Shuzenji Onsen Slow Day

**Theme: temple history, river-town atmosphere and time to enjoy the ryokan**

- **Morning — Kikuya breakfast:** The included ryokan breakfast is a composed Japanese meal rather than a quick buffet. It is part of the stay experience and removes any reason to rush out.
- **10:00 — Shuzenji Temple and Hie Shrine:** The Buddhist temple that gave the town its name and its neighbouring Shinto shrine form Shuzenji's historic and spiritual core. They earn the first stop because the onsen settlement grew around them.
- **11:30 — Tokko-no-Yu:** A small riverside pavilion marking Shuzenji's legendary hot-spring origin. It is worth a brief look for context, but it is a landmark only—bathing and foot bathing are not permitted.
- **12:00 — Bamboo Forest Path, Shigetsuden and Yoriie's grave:** A short riverside bamboo walk leads to a small repository and memorials tied to the exiled shogun Minamoto no Yoriie. Together they add atmosphere and Kamakura-period history without turning the slow day into a long hike.
- **13:00 — Local onsen-town lunch:** Choose soba (buckwheat noodles), a simple set meal or a café lunch. Keeping it flexible supports the purpose of the day—time in Shuzenji and at the ryokan rather than a distant Izu excursion.
- **14:30 — Kikuya baths and rest:** Kikuya is a historic ryokan, or traditional Japanese inn, arranged through linked corridors, gardens and bath areas. The return block earns its length because the baths and building—not just the room—are the central experience being paid for.
- **17:30 or 19:45 — Included Kikuya dinner:** A multi-course ryokan meal served as part of the half-board stay. It is a core experience rather than a separate restaurant hunt, so choose the seating at check-in.
- **After dinner — optional illuminated Bamboo Forest Path:** A quiet repeat of the short bamboo walk after dark. It is worthwhile only if you want a gentle post-dinner stroll; the official tourism association lists lighting until 23:00.

## Day 10 — Monday, Sep 28: Kyoto East — Temples & Traditions

**Theme: Eastern Kyoto temple circuit**

- **06:00 — Fushimi Inari Taisha at dawn:** The head shrine of Inari, the Shinto deity associated with rice and prosperity, famous for thousands of donated vermilion torii shrine gates climbing Mount Inari. It earns the exceptional early start because the gate tunnels are most atmospheric before tour groups arrive. Hike only to the Yotsutsuji intersection for city views; the Senbon Torii double rows appear near the beginning, so there is no need to summit.
- **08:00** Breakfast near Fushimi Inari or grab coffee on the way to next stop
- **09:30 — Kiyomizu-dera, Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka:** Kiyomizu-dera is a major hillside Buddhist temple whose wooden stage opens over eastern Kyoto; the preserved sloping lanes below retain traditional shopfronts and townscape. This cluster earns its place as the clearest single expression of temple architecture, city views and historic-street atmosphere.
- **11:00 — Kodai-ji or Yasaka Shrine:** Kodai-ji is a refined Zen memorial temple with gardens and links to Toyotomi Hideyoshi's widow; Yasaka is Gion's lively guardian shrine. Choose Kodai-ji for designed tranquillity or Yasaka for a shorter, more energetic stop—the alternative prevents temple overload.
- **12:00 — Yudofu lunch in Gion:** Yudofu is gently simmered tofu served with simple accompaniments, a Kyoto temple-cuisine speciality. Gion is the historic geiko district at the centre of this Higashiyama route, making it the natural place to pause. The meal earns its place as a distinctly local, light reset between temple stops.
- **13:30 — Philosopher's Path and Ginkaku-ji:** The Philosopher's Path is a two-kilometre canal walk linking quiet residential north-east Kyoto to Ginkaku-ji. The “Silver Pavilion” is valued less for silver than for its carefully composed dry garden, moss and villa landscape; together they provide a calmer counterweight to busy Kiyomizu.
- **15:30 — Nanzen-ji:** A major Zen temple complex with broad precincts and a striking nineteenth-century brick aqueduct crossing the grounds. It earns the final stop for the unusual collision of Zen landscape and industrial-era engineering; the aqueduct area is free to visit.
- **17:00** Return to hotel, rest
- **18:30 — Mid-range kaiseki dinner in Gion:** Kaiseki is a seasonal multi-course format balancing flavour, technique, tableware and presentation. A mid-range version earns one Kyoto evening because it demonstrates the city's formal cuisine without repeating the birthday dinner's full splurge.

## Day 11 — Tuesday, Sep 29: Uji + Tōfuku-ji

**Theme: Heian-era architecture, oldest extant Shinto shrine, Uji tea culture, modernist zen garden**

> 💡 By now you've done dawn Fushimi + Higashiyama temples (Mon), and Wed is the Kurama-Kibune mountain day. So Tue deliberately avoids more iconic-temple-bouncing and fills genuine gaps: the only Heian-era architecture on the trip (Byōdō-in, 1053), the oldest extant Shinto shrine (Ujigami, UNESCO), real tea culture, and Mirei Shigemori's 1939 modernist zen garden at Tōfuku-ji. Single JR Nara line geography.

- **10:00** Hotel departure. Walk to Kyoto Station. JR Nara line to **Uji** (~30 min local, ¥240, or ~17 min rapid)
- **11:00 — Byōdō-in Phoenix Hall:** An eleventh-century aristocratic Pure Land Buddhist temple whose symmetrical hall and reflecting pond appear on the ¥10 coin. It earns the journey to Uji as the trip's clearest surviving Heian-era architecture. The grounds ticket includes the **Hōshō-kan**, an underground-designed museum displaying the temple bell, phoenix ornaments, statues of Buddhist figures riding clouds and other original treasures—worth seeing for details that cannot be read from the hall's exterior. Grounds and museum ¥700; the hall interior tour is another ¥300 via limited same-day timed slots, so queue early. Allow about 75 minutes.
- **12:30 — Uji tea lunch:** Uji is one of Japan's defining tea centres. Choose **Tsuen Tea**, a centuries-old bridge-side teahouse, for continuity and atmosphere; **Nakamura Tokichi** for matcha-infused soba and desserts, accepting longer queues; or **Fukujuen Uji Workshop** to roast hōjicha, a nutty roasted green tea, yourself. This meal earns its place because tea is the town's living culture, not a souvenir theme. Budget ¥1,500–2,500 per person.
- **13:45 — Ujigami Jinja:** A small UNESCO-listed shrine preserving Japan's oldest extant Shinto shrine buildings. It is worth the short walk because its quiet scale and ancient timber architecture contrast sharply with Fushimi Inari's monumental gate landscape. Free; allow about 30 minutes.
- **14:30 — Uji River walk:** A calm riverside route with Tale of Genji literary connections. It connects the day's main sights without dead transit time and leaves room for an optional second tea tasting.
- **15:15** JR Nara line back toward Kyoto, alight at **Tōfuku-ji** (~15 min)
- **Approximately 15:40 — Tōfuku-ji:** One of Kyoto's Five Great Zen temples, best used here for Mirei Shigemori's 1939 Hōjō Garden, where chequerboard moss and stone recast the Zen garden in modernist form. It earns its place because it adds twentieth-century design to a day of ancient architecture. Hōjō garden ¥500. Entry closes at **16:00**, leaving roughly 50 minutes before the 16:30 closing; if the Uji morning slips, drop it.
- **17:00** Train back to Kyoto centre
- **~19:00 — Light dinner:** Choose **Pontocho**, the narrow lantern-lit restaurant alley by the Kamo River, for obanzai—seasonal Kyoto home cooking served as small dishes—or use the **Nishiki** food-market area for a simpler meal. Both sit conveniently in central Kyoto; this is intentionally not another splurge because the birthday dinner and two ryokan dinners already cover the trip's high-end meals.

**Critical timing:** Tōfuku-ji entry closes at 16:00, leaving a short focused visit if the Uji morning stays efficient. If the route slips, Tōfuku-ji is the drop; Uji alone is still a complete day. All stops are walk-up—no advance bookings needed.

## Day 12 — Wednesday, Sep 30: Kurama → Kibune Mountain Day

**Theme: ancient cedar forest, mountain-temple history and Kifune Shrine**

- **10:00** Walk/Keihan to Demachiyanagi → **Eizan Railway** to Kurama (~30 min, ¥470 pp)
- **10:50** Arrive **Kurama**. Optional cable car ¥200 to skip the first steep stretch, or walk the full path
- **11:00 — Yuki-jinja:** A small mountainside shrine reached through an old cedar avenue on the climb from Kurama. It earns a brief stop because the approach establishes the forest's sacred character before the main temple.
- **11:30 — Kurama-dera:** A mountain temple founded in the eighth century and wrapped in legends of tengu spirits and warrior training. It earns its place for pilgrimage atmosphere and deep-forest setting rather than ornate buildings alone.
- **12:15 — Kurama-to-Kibune trail:** A roughly one-hour forest crossing over the mountain, with mossy roots and ancient cedars. The hike is the day's central experience because it turns two temple villages into one coherent journey.
- **13:15 — Kifune Shrine:** A water deity shrine climbing the valley in lantern-lined stone terraces, with distinctive water-revealed fortunes. It is the natural end of the mountain crossing and adds a different, river-focused form of Shinto landscape.
- **13:45 — Kibune lunch:** Take a flexible late meal in the narrow riverside village. It belongs here as recovery after the trail; verify a suitable current restaurant and hours during the final pre-trip check rather than locking an unverified venue.
- **15:30** Walk to Kibuneguchi → Eizan Railway back toward Demachiyanagi (~30 min)
- **16:00 — Shimogamo Shrine:** One of Kyoto's oldest Shinto complexes, set within the ancient Tadasu no Mori woodland. It earns the stop because it is directly on the return route and provides formal shrine architecture in a flat, spacious forest after the rugged mountain sites.
- **17:00** Back to hotel area, rest
- **~19:00 — Light dinner:** Choose a depachika bento—a polished boxed meal from a department-store basement food hall—or soba, buckwheat noodles served hot or cold, near the hotel. Both are regional, efficient recovery food after the hike, not a second evening programme.

**Weather contingency:** The valley, temple and hike can work in light rain. In heavy rain, switch to **Daitoku-ji's sub-temples**, a compact cluster of Zen gardens and historic temple architecture, then **Walden Woods**, a minimalist speciality-coffee room that provides a deliberate indoor pause, followed by **Pontocho**, Kyoto's narrow lantern-lit riverside restaurant alley. This keeps the day's architecture and atmosphere without an exposed mountain trail. Decide morning-of from the JMA forecast.

## Day 13 — Thursday, Oct 1: Kyoto → Nara → Osaka

**Theme: Kyoto checkout, Nara's giant Buddha + deer, then settle into Osaka**

- **10:00** Check out of the Kyoto hotel with the small travelling bag. The main suitcase should already be with Yamato en route to Haneda Terminal 3, having been handed over on Sep 30—or Sep 29 if the route-specific cutoff required it
- **10:30** Train to **Nara** (~45 min, ¥720 by JR or Kintetsu)
- **11:30 — Nara Park:** A broad historic park where free-roaming deer are treated as sacred messengers. The deer provide atmosphere and connect the major temple sites, but keep food away except when deliberately offering official crackers.
- **12:00 — Tōdai-ji:** A monumental Buddhist temple whose Great Buddha Hall shelters one of Japan's largest bronze Buddha images. It earns the Nara stop for sheer scale and for the political-religious ambition of eighth-century Japan.
- **13:00 — Nara lunch:** Try kakinoha-zushi, pressed sushi wrapped in persimmon leaf for preservation, or use Higashimuki's covered shopping street for a quick set meal. The regional sushi is the more distinctive choice; the arcade is the efficient weatherproof fallback.
- **14:00 — Kasuga Taisha:** Nara's great vermilion Shinto shrine, approached through a forest of donated stone lanterns and filled with bronze lanterns inside. It earns its place as a more intimate, wooded counterpart to Tōdai-ji's monumental Buddhism.
- **15:30** Train from Nara to **Osaka Namba** (~40 min, ¥570)
- **16:00** Check into **Via Inn Prime Shinsaibashi Yotsubashi** (1-5-10 Shinmachi, Nishi-ku — Yotsubashi station, 1 min). From Osaka-Namba, hop the Yotsubashi line 1 stop to Yotsubashi, or 12-min walk west. Drop bags. *(AEON supermarket on the ground floor for snacks/water.)*
- **17:00 — Dotonbori and takoyaki:** Dotonbori is Osaka's neon canal-side entertainment strip, centred on animated signs and the **Glico Running Man**, the illuminated running-athlete billboard that has become the district's best-known meeting point and visual emblem. It earns the first-evening wander for maximum city energy; try takoyaki—soft-centred octopus batter balls—at established specialists Wanaka or Kogaryu. Then pass through **Shinsaibashi-suji**, the long covered retail arcade linking Dotonbori to the hotel side of central Osaka; it adds local shopping bustle while naturally completing the route.
- **19:00 — Okonomiyaki dinner:** Osaka-style okonomiyaki is a savoury cabbage pancake cooked on a griddle with meat or seafood, sauce and bonito flakes. It belongs on the list as one of the city's signature communal comfort foods; choose Fukutaro for the convenient Namba location or Michelin-listed Mizuno for the classic Dotonbori institution, accepting its usual queue.
- **20:30 — Dotonbori after dark:** Return to the canal once the signs are fully lit. The night view is the point; optional kushikatsu—breaded, deep-fried skewers—at a standing bar only if still hungry.

## Day 14 — Friday, Oct 2: Osaka Deep Dive

**Theme: Street-food capital and local neighbourhoods**

- **09:30 — Kuromon Market:** A covered food market historically known as “Osaka's kitchen”, now increasingly tourist-oriented and expensive. It merits a quick atmospheric walk-through, but save appetite and money for Shinsekai rather than treating it as the day's food destination.
- **10:30 — Shinsekai:** A proudly retro entertainment district beneath Tsutenkaku Tower, mixing old signboards, cheap eateries and working-class Osaka character. It earns its place as the rougher, more local contrast to polished Dotonbori.
  - **Kushikatsu at Daruma:** Breaded skewers of meat, seafood and vegetables, fried to order. This is Shinsekai's signature food and Daruma is closely associated with the style; follow the current sauce instructions rather than assuming the old communal-pot system.
  - **Janjan Yokocho:** A narrow covered alley of small eateries, game parlours and bars. Walk it for concentrated retro atmosphere rather than a specific attraction.
- **12:00 — Shinsekai lunch:** Continue with kushikatsu if sharing a broad selection, or switch to ramen—wheat noodles in a savoury broth—at a local shop. Staying in the district keeps the route efficient and lets the neighbourhood, not a reservation, drive the meal.
- **13:30 — Osaka Castle:** A modern reconstruction housing a museum within the vast stone walls and moats of the Toyotomi-era fortress site. The grounds and exterior earn the stop for scale and political history; treat the interior museum as optional if energy is fading.
- **15:00 — Nakazakicho:** A low-rise pre-war neighbourhood repurposed with independent cafés, vintage shops and small creative businesses. It earns its place as an intimate, adaptive-reuse contrast to Osaka's big commercial districts.
- **16:30 — Tenjinbashi-suji:** A very long covered shopping arcade serving everyday local life. Browse, snack and people-watch here for non-tourist commercial atmosphere rather than landmark sightseeing.
- **18:00** Back to Namba
- **19:00 — Tachinomi crawl:** Tachinomi are compact standing bars built around inexpensive drinks and small plates such as karaage (Japanese fried chicken), grilled fish and whisky-and-soda highballs. Two or three near Namba make a fitting final dinner because the format is social, flexible and distinctly urban Osaka without requiring another long sit-down meal.
- **21:00 — Optional farewell Dotonbori walk:** Revisit the neon canal after the standing-bar dinner only if one last burst of Osaka energy appeals. It is a mood-setting finale, not another attraction to complete.
