# Practical Information

## Weather — Late September / Early October
- **Temperature:** 20-28°C (68-82°F), gradually cooling
- **Humidity:** Decreasing from summer but still noticeable
- **Rain:** Occasional — typhoon season technically runs through October but risk drops significantly in late September
- **What to pack:** Light layers, one light rain jacket, comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk 15,000-25,000 steps/day), umbrella

## Money & Payments
- **Currency:** Japanese Yen (¥). Bring some GBP/USD to exchange at the airport, or withdraw from 7-Eleven/Japan Post ATMs (Visa/Mastercard accepted)
- **Cash is important:** Many smaller restaurants, temples, and rural spots are cash-only. Carry ¥10,000-20,000 cash at all times
- **IC cards (Suica/PASMO):** Use for trains, buses, and konbini purchases. Set up Mobile Suica on iPhone for maximum convenience
- **Credit cards:** Accepted at hotels, department stores, larger restaurants, and chain shops
- **Kikuya balance:** ¥165,600 is payable on site. The confirmation says cash or credit card only; electronic and QR-code payments are not accepted

## Connectivity
- **eSIM** (recommended): Ubigi or Airalo — buy and activate before departure, ~¥2,000-5,000 for 14 days
- **Pocket WiFi** (alternative): Rent online, pick up at airport. More reliable in rural areas
- **Free WiFi:** Available at stations, konbini, and some cafes but unreliable

## Getting Around
- **Trains are king.** Tokyo Metro, JR lines, private railways cover everything
- **Google Maps** is the primary tool for live transit and walking routes; downloaded maps do not provide offline transit or walking directions
- **SmartEX** works in the mobile browser. Save the separate QR ticket for each passenger and print both as backup
- **Taxis:** GO is the main local dispatch app. If foreign-number registration is blocked outside Japan, register after landing with location enabled and the home SIM able to receive the SMS code; keep Uber as the immediate fallback
- **Walking:** Many neighborhoods are best explored on foot. Wear comfortable shoes
- **Shuzenji transfer:** **Odoriko 9, Tokyo 12:00 → Shuzenji 14:06** is a paid NAVITIME application awaiting ticket arrangement and paper-ticket collection details. Then take the Tokai Bus for approximately eight minutes and alight at **Shuzenji Onsen**. There is no hotel shuttle. The Tokyo→Mishima→Shuzenji route is the disruption fallback
- **Luggage forwarding:** use two prepaid Yamato sends: Hotel Monterey Akasaka → Kyoto at checkout on Sep 25, then Kyoto → Haneda Airport on Sep 29 or 30. The main case therefore bypasses Shuzenji, Nara, Osaka and the final Shinkansen

## 🧳 Luggage Forwarding — Complete Guide

### What the service is

Japan’s luggage-forwarding network is a domestic parcel service, not a railway baggage service. The most useful operator for this trip is **Yamato Transport’s TA-Q-BIN (宅急便 / takkyūbin)**. A staffed hotel can complete the waybill, take payment and hand the suitcase to Yamato; the receiving hotel or airport counter holds it for collection.

Normal forwarding is **not same-day**. Yamato says hotel-bound luggage must be handed over before the local cutoff at least two days before check-in, with three days sometimes required for longer routes. Airport luggage normally needs two days, or three from some origins. Same-day luggage services exist only on selected city routes and should not be treated as a rescue plan.

Official information: [standard TA-Q-BIN](https://www.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/ytc/en/send/services/takkyubin/) · [hotel delivery rules](https://faq-en.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/6692) · [Airport TA-Q-BIN](https://www.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/ytc/en/send/services/airport/)

**Alternatives:** Yamato is the best fit here because both planned sends involve staffed hotels and the second ends at its Haneda counter. **Sagawa Express** is useful when a case exceeds Yamato’s limit: its large-size service accepts parcels through 260cm/50kg. **Japan Post Yu-Pack** accepts ordinary parcels through 170cm/25kg; Weighted Yu-Pack handles 25-30kg at post offices. Do not switch carriers casually for the airport leg—confirm that the exact departure terminal has the correct receiving counter and lead time.

Official alternatives: [Sagawa services](https://www.sagawa-exp.co.jp/english/service/) · [Japan Post Yu-Pack](https://www.post.japanpost.jp/service/you_pack/index_en.html)

### Size, weight and likely cost

- Measure **length + width + height**, including wheels and handles. Yamato uses whichever produces the higher charge: dimensional class or weight class.
- Maximum standard and Airport TA-Q-BIN size: **200cm total and 30kg**; generally no single side may exceed 170cm.
- Common large-case classes: **Size 140, up to 20kg**; **Size 160, up to 25kg**. A 23kg case measuring under 140cm is charged as Size 160 because weight controls.
- Convenience stores accept suitcases only through **Size 160**. A hotel desk or Yamato office is safer for a large case.
- Published planning fares before any hotel fee: Tokyo → Kyoto about **£10.90 (¥2,310)** at Size 140 or **£12.40 (¥2,630)** at Size 160. Kyoto → Haneda, including Yamato’s ¥660 airport fee, is about **£14.00 (¥2,970)** or **£15.50 (¥3,290)**.
- Both sends together should be approximately **£24.90–27.90 (¥5,280–5,920)** for one Size 140–160 case. The hotel’s measured quote is final; the trip budget carries a conservative **£32 (¥6,000)** allowance.

### How to send hotel-to-hotel

1. **Confirm both ends.** The sending hotel must handle Yamato and the receiving hotel must agree to accept and hold the case. This is not automatic at every hotel, hostel or apartment.
2. Ask for **prepaid service — 元払い / motobarai**. Yamato does not permit shipping-charge cash on delivery for hotel-bound luggage.
3. Supply the destination hotel’s exact name, postcode, address and telephone number. Add the guest name exactly as booked, check-in date and reservation number if requested.
4. Request arrival at least one day before check-in. Hotel deliveries do not offer a selectable delivery-time window.
5. Fully close the zip and lock the case if possible. A cover is optional; Yamato treats the suitcase shell as its packaging, so cosmetic marks are possible.
6. Photograph the case, completed waybill and tracking number. Keep the customer copy until the suitcase is physically back with you.
7. Track it using Yamato’s [English Parcel Enquiry System](https://track.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/english/tracking).

Useful phrase at reception:

> **Takkyūbin de, tsugi no hoteru made okuritai desu. Motobarai de onegaishimasu.**
>
> I’d like to send this by TA-Q-BIN to my next hotel, prepaid please.

### Never put these in the forwarded case

Carry passports, cash, bank cards, essential medication, keys, tickets, valuables, irreplaceable documents and everything needed before the promised arrival date. Yamato also prohibits dangerous, flammable or explosive items and parcels worth more than ¥300,000. Keep power banks and mission-critical electronics with you rather than risking a restriction or delay.

Pack the small travelling bag as though the large case could arrive one day late: underwear, sleepwear, four days of clothes, toiletries, medication, chargers, rain layer and one presentable outfit. Never put the airport-waybill receipt inside the forwarded suitcase.

Official restrictions: [dangerous and prohibited items](https://www.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/ytc/en/send/preparations/inability/) · [sending a suitcase](https://faq-en.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/4007)

### Transfer 2 — Kyoto → Haneda, bypassing Nara and Osaka

This is the highest-value send: it removes the large suitcase from the Nara stopover, Osaka hotel transfer, Midōsuji Line, Shin-Osaka, Tokyo Station and Haneda connection.

**Confirm at Kyoto check-in on Sep 27:** ask the front desk to check the route-specific Yamato **Airport TA-Q-BIN** deadline from the hotel to Haneda. Obtain a clear yes/no that a case handed over on **Sep 30 before the carrier cutoff** will be available on Oct 3. If not, send it on **Sep 29 before the cutoff**.

**Use the dedicated Airport TA-Q-BIN waybill**, not an ordinary hotel form. Enter:

- **Haneda Airport — Terminal 3**
- Departure date: **3 Oct 2026**
- Airline/flight: **Air China CA168**
- Scheduled departure: **19:15**, if still shown on the live booking
- Passenger name exactly as on the passport
- Reachable telephone number

Confirm CA168’s live time and terminal before completing the form. Keep the sender receipt and tracking number in the small bag; check tracking on Oct 1 and Oct 2.

**Collect on Oct 3:** Yamato receipt counter, **Haneda Terminal 3, 2F Arrival Lobby**, currently 06:00–23:00. Yamato says airport luggage can be collected one to three hours before boarding; if CA168 remains 19:15, target **16:15**. Present the sender receipt, collect the suitcase and take it to Air China check-in. Yamato delivers only to the airport counter—it does not check the suitcase onto the flight.

Official counter details: [Haneda home-delivery counters](https://tokyo-haneda.com/en/service/facilities/delivery.html)

### Fallback if Kyoto → Haneda cannot be confirmed

Via Inn’s [official FAQ](https://www.viainn.com/shinsaibashi-y/faq/) confirms advance receipt through check-in day and refuses cash-on-delivery, valuables, fragile goods and temperature-controlled items.

Collect it in Osaka and take it on the Oct 3 Shinkansen. If length + width + height is **161–250cm**, reserve a seat with an oversized-baggage area/compartment; at 160cm or below, that special reservation is not required. This works, but it is materially worse than direct Kyoto → Haneda.

### If something goes wrong

- Check the tracking record first.
- Yamato English Customer Service: **0120-67-9625** in Japan or **+81-3-6834-7503**, currently 08:00–21:00 daily.
- If a hotel cannot locate the case, show reception the waybill photo and tracking record, then confirm the booking-name spelling and delivery date.

## Etiquette Basics
- **Bowing:** A slight bow is appreciated when greeting, thanking, or saying goodbye
- **Shoes off:** Remove shoes when entering ryokans, some restaurants, temples, and homes. Look for a genkan (entryway) and slippers
- **Quiet on trains:** Keep voices low, phones on silent, no phone calls
- **Tipping:** Do NOT tip. It's not expected and can be confusing
- **Queueing:** Japanese queue culture is strong. Line up neatly
- **Chopstick rules:** Don't stick chopsticks upright in rice (funeral association), don't pass food chopstick-to-chopstick

## Onsen Etiquette (Yukairou Kikuya, Shuzenji)
1. Wash thoroughly at the shower stations BEFORE entering the bath
2. No swimwear in the onsen — you go in naked
3. Tie long hair up so it doesn't touch the water
4. Small towel can go on your head but NOT in the water
5. Be quiet and relaxed — onsen is for contemplation
6. **Tattoo policy is unverified:** Kikuya publishes no explicit rule. Its four private baths do not prove that tattoos are accepted in the communal baths. Ask the hotel directly before relying on communal bathing

## Kikuya Booking Practicalities
- **Room:** 35m² Japanese-style room with two individual futons; the booking's “twin” label refers to the futon arrangement, not Western twin beds
- **Meals:** dinner and breakfast included on both nights; dinner sitting chosen at check-in (17:30 or 19:45)
- **Dietary requests:** the confirmation asks for allergy-menu applications by two days before arrival. It describes a low-allergen menu covering Japan's specified 28 allergens, does not offer single-item substitutions, and cannot guarantee against cross-contamination
- **Cancellation schedule printed in the email:** no-show 100%; same day 80%; 2 days before 50%; 4 days before 20%; 7 days before 10%. The email says plan-specific fees may differ

## 🗑️ Trash Disposal — No Public Bins!

**There are almost no public trash bins in Japan.** This surprises every first-timer.

**What to do:**
- Carry a small plastic bag in your daypack for your trash
- Convenience stores (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) have trash bins — use them when you buy something there
- Vending machines have recycling bins attached (bottles/cans only)
- Train stations sometimes have bins near the gates
- Your hotel room will have a trash bin

**Recycling matters:** When you do find bins, they're separated into:
- Burnable (paper, food waste)
- PET bottles (plastic drink bottles)
- Cans / Glass bottles
- Non-burnable (plastics)

### Finding a casual dinner

Search the exact dish near the day’s endpoint in Google Maps, shortlist two or three places, then inspect recent menu photos and the newest substantive reviews. Cross-check the restaurant on Tabelog’s English site: its score is weighted rather than a simple average, and 3.50–3.99 is already approximately the top 3%, so 3.5 is a strong positive rather than a minimum cutoff. Verify current opening and last-order times through the restaurant’s official channel or live booking page.

## 👟 Shoe Removal Protocol

Remove shoes when:
- **Entering any home** (including Airbnb)
- **Entering ryokan rooms** (change to indoor slippers at the entrance)
- **Entering some temples** (look for shoe racks, raised floors, or signs)
- **Entering some restaurants** (especially tatami-seated ones)
- **Entering fitting rooms** at some stores
- **Entering some onsen changing rooms**

**The general rule:** If the floor level changes (steps up), shoes come off. Look for a **genkan** (entry area with a level change) and a shoe rack.

**⚠️ Toilet slippers:** Many ryokans and some restaurants have separate slippers for the toilet room. Wear them IN the toilet, remove them when you leave. Forgetting to switch back is the classic tourist mistake.

**Tip:** Wear slip-on shoes — you'll be removing them constantly at temples and restaurants. Laces become annoying very quickly.

## 🚂 Additional Train Etiquette

Beyond keeping quiet:
- **No eating on local trains** (commuter trains, subway). Eating IS acceptable on Shinkansen — buy an ekiben (station bento)
- **Queue in marked lines** on the platform. People line up precisely at door markers
- **Let passengers exit first** before boarding
- **Backpacks:** Take off large backpacks and hold them low or place on luggage rack
- **Priority seats** (marked in different color at end of each car): yield to elderly, pregnant, disabled, small children

### 🧳 Shinkansen Oversized Baggage Rule

On the Tokaido–Sanyo Shinkansen (Tokyo ↔ Kyoto ↔ Osaka):
- The planned main suitcase is forwarded around both relevant Shinkansen legs, so these rules matter only if the luggage plan changes
- Bags exceeding **160cm total dimensions** (length + width + height) must be stored in an **"oversized baggage area"** at the back of designated cars
- You must **reserve a seat with oversized baggage** when booking your ticket (through SmartEX in the browser or at the station)
- There is **no extra charge** for the oversized area — you just need to reserve the right seat type
- Standard carry-on suitcases (~157cm) usually fit under the limit, but **measure yours before travelling**
- Bringing oversized baggage without a reservation may result in a ¥1,000 fee

## 🎌 Cultural Tips Beyond Etiquette

- **No eating while walking** (except at markets and festivals). Find a spot to stand and eat
- **"Itadakimasu"** — say this before eating at a restaurant. It means "I humbly receive." Staff genuinely appreciate it
- **"Sumimasen"** is the single most useful word. Use it to get attention, apologize, thank, or excuse yourself
- **Umbrella etiquette:** When entering a store/restaurant, look for the umbrella stand or plastic umbrella bags at the entrance
- **Escalator standing:** Stand on the LEFT in Tokyo, LEFT in Kyoto/Osaka (despite the common myth about Osaka being different, it varies)
- **Konbini payment:** Place money on the tray, not directly in the cashier's hand
